02 / SPATIAL MEASUREMENT

When a phone becomes a measuring instrument.

A current iPhone carries a depth sensor, a good camera and a motion stack accurate enough to measure the physical world, provided the pipeline between them is built with some care.

The problem

The demo is easy. An overlay that sits convincingly in a room takes an afternoon. Producing a number somebody will act on is a different job, especially from a handheld device, in poor light, held by a person who isn't thinking about tracking quality.

How a measurement is produced

  1. 01

    CAPTURE

    Camera frames and depth samples arrive together, timestamped against the same clock so they can be correlated later.

  2. 02

    DEPTH MAP

    The raw map is sparse and noisy. Dark or absorbent surfaces return nothing at all, and edges scatter badly.

  3. 03

    SPATIAL MODEL

    Planes and anchors are fitted across frames. Samples that disagree with their neighbours are thrown away rather than averaged in.

  4. 04

    MEASUREMENT

    A distance is derived, and a confidence value is derived alongside it. The second number matters as much as the first.

  5. 05

    INSIGHT

    Above the threshold it becomes a figure someone can act on. Below it, the honest answer is to ask for another scan.

The approach

Depth data comes in noisy and full of holes. Some surfaces swallow the signal, edges scatter it, and quick movement smears frames together. Most of the work is rejection: deciding what to trust, what to throw away, and when to ask the operator to scan again rather than hand back a confident wrong answer.

Vision and on-device models narrow it further by classifying what's in frame, so the measurement runs against the right surface. All of it stays on the phone, which keeps latency down and means the imagery never leaves the device.

Anyone can float a box in a room. The hard bit is knowing when your number is good enough to act on.

The part that took the longest

Deciding when to refuse. It is easy to always return a number. Knowing which numbers are not good enough to hand over is the whole problem.

What the work involved

  • The constraint

    A handheld device, poor light, and an operator not thinking about tracking quality.

  • The engineering

    Depth capture, plane detection, aggressive sample rejection and on-device classification.

  • The ownership

    The sensor pipeline, the confidence model and the capture experience.

  • The outcome

    A number with confidence attached, or an honest prompt to scan again.
