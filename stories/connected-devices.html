05 / CONNECTED DEVICES

When software has to listen to the physical world.

Bluetooth peripherals and on-device sensors throw off a constant stream of readings, and not one of them is trustworthy on its own.

  • CORE BLUETOOTH
  • BLE
  • SENSOR STREAMS
  • REAL-TIME
  • SIGNAL PROCESSING

The problem

Signals drop. Devices vanish mid-session and come back with a different identifier. Readings turn up out of order, duplicated, or stamped with a clock that disagrees with the phone. Taken alone, any single one is a poor basis for a decision.

How a decision gets made

  1. 01

    RAW STREAMS

    Readings arrive late, duplicated, out of order, and sometimes from a device that has reconnected under a new identifier.

  2. 02

    TIME ALIGNMENT

    Everything is buffered into a common window and stamped against one clock, so the streams can be compared at all.

  3. 03

    CROSS-CHECK

    Sources are compared against each other. Agreement raises confidence, and disagreement is recorded rather than smoothed away.

  4. 04

    DECISION

    Acted on only when enough independent sources concur. A single reading is never enough on its own.

The approach

So nothing acts on one reading. Streams get buffered, lined up in time, and checked against each other. When independent sources agree, confidence goes up. When they don't, that's useful too: it usually means a sensor has drifted or a connection is on its way out.

The connection lifecycle ends up being most of the work. Reconnect cleanly, pick up without losing the window, and never let a dropout look to the user like a wrong answer.

One sensor gives you a reading. Several that agree give you something you can act on.

The part that took the longest

The connection lifecycle. Reconnecting cleanly, resuming without losing the window, and never letting a dropout look like a wrong answer.

What the work involved

  • The constraint

    Readings arrive late, duplicated, or from a device that just reconnected.

  • The engineering

    Buffering, time alignment, cross-source agreement and connection lifecycle handling.

  • The ownership

    The stream pipeline, confidence scoring and reconnect behaviour.

  • The outcome

    Decisions backed by several sources rather than one hopeful reading.
